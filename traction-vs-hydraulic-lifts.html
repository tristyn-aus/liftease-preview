Commercial lifts compared

Traction vs hydraulic lifts for commercial buildings

Quick answer

For offices, apartments, clinics and other busy buildings, traction is the stronger choice. A traction lift moves the car with ropes or belts over a motor-driven sheave, usually with a counterweight, and handles high cycle counts and higher speeds well.

That is why every Lift Ease commercial passenger elevator is electric traction. We don’t offer hydraulic commercial passenger lifts, because traction has a superior duty cycle and long-lasting reliability in high-use commercial settings.

Why duty cycle matters in a commercial building

Duty cycle is how hard a lift works: how many trips it makes in an hour and across a day. A home lift might make a handful of trips a day. A lift in an office, clinic or apartment block can run almost constantly at peak times.

Traction drives are designed for that kind of continuous use, and VVVF control keeps starts and stops smooth while managing the motor efficiently. That combination of duty cycle and long-lasting reliability is why we only install electric traction for commercial passenger elevators.

Where hydraulic fits

Hydraulic lifts are proven and smooth, and many older low-rise commercial buildings still run them. Hydraulic is a good fit where duty is low: homes, which is why our Vector Pro home elevator is hydraulic, and access lifts, which is why our RB150 platform lift is available with a hydraulic variant (its standard drive is electric screw).

If your building already has a hydraulic lift, our technicians service and repair hydraulic systems, including checks on fluid levels, pressure systems and seals. When it is time to replace it, we will quote an electric traction lift.

Choosing

Which is right for your building?

Choose electric traction if

  • The lift will be used often, all day, by staff, residents, patients or the public.
  • You need speeds from 0.3 m/s up to 2.0 m/s, or capacity up to 2,500 kg.
  • You want a DDA-compliant 1100 × 1400 mm car (Zenith DDA).
  • You want long-lasting reliability with lower oil-related maintenance.

Hydraulic may still make sense if

  • Your building already has a hydraulic lift that is in good condition. We can service and repair it.
  • It is a private home rather than a commercial building. See our Vector Pro.
  • You need a low-duty platform access lift rather than a passenger elevator. See our RB150.

Common misconceptions

Myths debunked

  • Myth A commercial lift always needs three phase power.

    Fact Not always. The Zenith DDA commercial passenger elevator and the RB150 platform lift run on single phase 230VAC. The E1, built for higher speeds and loads, uses three phase power.

  • Myth Every lift needs a machine room.

    Fact Many modern lifts are machine-room-less, with the drive inside the shaft or lift tower. The Atlus is a fully contained screw system, and even our hydraulic Vector Pro needs only a small cupboard for its pump, with no machine room.

  • Myth Installing a lift takes months.

    Fact Once the site is ready, a Lift Ease home lift typically installs in 1 to 2 weeks, a Levare in about 2 days, an RB150 platform lift within a week, and a commercial passenger elevator in 2 to 4 weeks. Design, approvals and building works come first and vary by project.

FAQs

Questions we get asked

Why does Lift Ease only install traction commercial lifts?

Because electric traction has a superior duty cycle and long-lasting reliability in high-use commercial settings. We don’t offer hydraulic commercial passenger lifts.

Is traction more energy efficient than hydraulic?

In general, yes. A traction lift’s counterweight balances much of the car’s weight, so the motor only has to move the difference. A hydraulic lift has to push the whole car up. Actual use depends on traffic and the building.

How fast are your commercial lifts?

The Zenith DDA runs at 0.3 to 1.0 m/s and the E1 at 1.0 to 2.0 m/s.

Do I need three phase power for a commercial lift?

Not always. The Zenith DDA runs on single phase 230VAC. The E1 uses three phase power.

Can you service our existing hydraulic lift?

Yes. Our technicians service and repair hydraulic systems, including fluid levels, pressure systems and seals, and lifts from all major manufacturers.

How long does a commercial lift take to install?

Our Zenith DDA and E1 passenger elevators typically take 2 to 4 weeks once the site is ready, and the RB150 platform access lift is installed within a week. Design, approvals and builder’s works come first.
