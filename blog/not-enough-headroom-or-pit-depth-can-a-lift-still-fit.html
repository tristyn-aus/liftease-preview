Not Enough Headroom or Pit Depth? How a Lift Can Still Fit Your Building

9 November 2026

Short answer: a tight space doesn’t automatically rule out a lift. The Vector Pro and Atlus need 2300 mm of headroom (with a 2100 mm reduced headroom model), the Atlus needs a pit of only 50 mm to 150 mm, and the Zenith DDA suits pits of 150 mm to 1200 mm. If your building still doesn’t match, bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available, so ask for a feasibility assessment before you decide it can’t be done.

Headroom (the clear height above the top floor level) and pit depth (the recess below the bottom floor) are the two measurements that most often worry Victorian homeowners, builders and architects. Here’s how our lifts compare.

Figures are the published specifications for each model. Bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available on top of these, so treat them as a starting point rather than a hard limit.

Home lifts for tight spaces

If headroom is the problem, the Vector Pro and Atlus are the models to look at first. Both need 2300 mm, and each has a reduced headroom model at 2100 mm. Neither needs a machine room, and the Vector Pro’s pump fits in a small cupboard.

If a deep pit is the problem, the Atlus has a low to no pit option of 50 mm to 150 mm, which helps where digging is difficult. It also serves up to 5 stops over up to 12 m of travel. The Vector Pro needs a 120 mm pit.

The Zenith Ultra needs more headroom (2700 mm to 3200 mm), which is the trade-off for its glass design features and larger cabin. It can also be fitted with all six door types, whereas the Vector Pro and Atlus use swing doors only. Our comparison of Vector Pro, Zenith Ultra and Atlus goes through the rest of the differences.

Commercial and DDA lifts in existing buildings

Existing commercial buildings rarely have an ideal shaft or a deep slab. The Zenith DDA is fully DDA and NCC compliant, and it suits a pit of 150 mm to 1200 mm with headroom of 2700 mm to 3200 mm. Because it’s machine-room-less, you also avoid finding space for a machine room. Our guide to Zenith DDA, E1 and RB150 covers how the commercial lifts compare, and DDA elevators in Melbourne explains what compliance involves.

Why “no machine room” matters when space is tight

Traditional lifts put their motor and drive in a separate room above or beside the shaft. Every Lift Ease unit is machine-room-less, so the equipment sits inside the shaft or lift tower and you don’t have to plan, build or give up space for a room. See MRL vs machine room lifts for more.

What a feasibility assessment involves

If your measurements fall outside the figures above, don’t give up. We’re flexible, and bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available. To get started:

  1. Gather your plans, or rough measurements of the floor-to-floor height, the space above the top floor and the space below the bottom floor.
  2. Request a feasibility assessment and tell us whether the project is a home or a commercial building.
  3. We’ll confirm which model and options suit the space. For help planning ahead, see our home lift planning guide.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

How much headroom does a home elevator need?

The Vector Pro and Atlus need 2300 mm, and both have a reduced headroom model at 2100 mm. The Zenith Ultra needs 2700 mm to 3200 mm.

How deep does the pit need to be?

It depends on the model. The Atlus needs 50 mm to 150 mm, the Vector Pro 120 mm and the Zenith Ultra 150 mm to 300 mm. The commercial Zenith DDA needs 150 mm to 1200 mm.

Do I need a machine room as well?

No. Every Lift Ease lift is machine-room-less, so you don't need to find or build a machine room. The Vector Pro's pump fits in a small cupboard.

What if my building doesn't match the standard figures?

Bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available. Send us your plans and we'll do a feasibility assessment before you rule anything out.

Does a tight space affect the warranty?

Our full elevators carry a Factory backed 5 Year Parts Warranty.

Do you cover areas outside Melbourne?

We work mainly across Victoria, plus selected projects in NSW, SA and Tasmania.
