Zenith DDA, E1 or RB150: Which Commercial Lift Does Your Building Need?

2 November 2026

Short answer: choose the Zenith DDA for most offices, medical centres, schools, retail and small apartment buildings that need a full passenger lift for up to 10 levels on single phase power. Choose the E1 for larger or busier buildings that need up to 2,500 kg, up to 35 m of travel or goods configurations. Choose the RB150 platform lift when you only need to bridge a short rise of up to 3,000 mm and your building surveyor confirms a platform lift is acceptable.

All three carry our Factory backed 5 Year Parts Warranty. Here’s how they compare for Victorian buildings.

Choose the Zenith DDA for most commercial buildings

The Zenith DDA is the public-use version of our Zenith, built specifically for high-traffic commercial environments. Its electric traction drive handles high cycle counts while keeping a sleek, professional office design, and it’s fully DDA and NCC compliant.

It serves up to 10 levels (G+9) at 0.3 to 1.0 m/s with a 1100 × 1400 mm car. Because it’s machine-room-less and runs on single phase 230VAC power, it avoids two common headaches in existing buildings: finding space for a machine room and upgrading to three phase power.

Choose the E1 for bigger loads and taller buildings

The E1 is designed for commercial buildings, apartments, offices, healthcare facilities, hotels and public spaces in low and mid-rise developments. Its VVVF traction drive runs at 1.0 to 2.0 m/s, it carries up to 2,500 kg, travels up to 35 m and comes in passenger and goods configurations with automatic centre-opening or side-opening doors.

The trade-off is power and pit: the E1 is the only Lift Ease lift that needs three phase power, and it needs a 1200 mm pit. Like the Zenith DDA, it’s machine-room-less.

Choose the RB150 for a short rise

Not every access problem needs a full enclosed cabin. The RB150 is a modern open platform lift with a lifting height of 600 mm to 3,000 mm. It’s DDA compliant, fits comfortably into tight spaces, needs minimal to no construction and is installed within a week, which makes it a cost-effective way to get a wheelchair user up a few steps at a school, clinic or shopfront.

Platform lifts do have limits under the National Construction Code, including where another type of passenger lift could be installed. Our guide to DDA elevators in Melbourne explains the rules, and your building surveyor or access consultant should confirm the lift type before you order.

Why traction for commercial passenger lifts?

We only install electric traction for our commercial passenger elevators, because of its superior duty cycle and long-lasting reliability in high-use commercial settings. We don’t offer hydraulic commercial passenger lifts. Our traction vs hydraulic lifts comparison explains the difference, and machine-room-less vs machine room lifts covers why we don’t need a machine room.

Tight shaft or shallow slab?

Existing buildings rarely have the ideal shaft. Bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available, so don’t rule a lift out before we’ve looked at it. Send us your plans and we’ll do a feasibility assessment.

Servicing after handover

A commercial lift has to keep running. Our elevator servicing team has years of experience across all major brands, and our servicing includes built-in 24/7 SIM monitoring, which keeps an eye on the elevator’s health around the clock so issues can be found and fixed early.

Next step

Get a free quote with details of your project and our commercial estimators will prepare a tailored proposal, typically within 48 hours. We work mainly across Victoria, plus selected projects in NSW, SA and Tasmania. Builders and architects can also see our lifts for architects and designers.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Which Lift Ease lift suits a small office or medical centre?

Usually the Zenith DDA. It's built for high-traffic commercial and office buildings, serves up to 10 levels (G+9), runs on single phase 230VAC power and is fully DDA and NCC compliant.

When should I choose the E1 instead?

When you need more speed, capacity or travel: the E1 runs at 1.0 to 2.0 m/s, carries up to 2,500 kg, travels up to 35 m and comes in passenger and goods configurations. It needs three phase power.

Is a platform lift enough for DDA access?

Sometimes. The RB150 is a DDA compliant open platform lift with a lifting height of 600 mm to 3,000 mm, suited to short rises in schools, clinics and public buildings. Platform lifts have limits under the NCC, so confirm the lift type with your building surveyor or access consultant first.

Do your commercial lifts need a machine room?

No. The Zenith DDA and E1 are both machine-room-less, so no separate machine room is needed.

What pit depth does a commercial lift need?

The Zenith DDA needs a pit of 150 mm to 1200 mm and headroom of 2700 mm to 3200 mm. The E1 needs a 1200 mm pit. Bespoke sizing, reduced headroom and reduced pit depths are available, so ask for a feasibility assessment if your building is tight.

How long does installation take?

The Zenith DDA and E1 typically take 2 to 4 weeks to install, and the RB150 is installed within a week.

What warranty and servicing do you offer?

All three carry our Factory backed 5 Year Parts Warranty. We also service and repair lifts from all major manufacturers, and our servicing includes built-in 24/7 SIM monitoring.
