Retrofitting a Home Elevator in Melbourne: What Homeowners Need to Know

19 June 2026

Melbourne is a city of wonderfully diverse homes — Victorian terraces in Fitzroy, Federation-era houses in Hawthorn, 1960s brick veneer across the eastern suburbs, and sleek modern townhouses springing up throughout the inner north and south. What many of these homes have in common is that they were built long before residential elevators were even a consideration. But that’s changing fast.

More Melbourne homeowners are now retrofitting home elevators into their existing properties — not just for accessibility and aging in place, but as a practical quality-of-life upgrade and a genuine boost to property value. If you’ve been wondering whether your current home can accommodate a residential lift, the short answer is: it almost certainly can. Here’s what the process looks like.

What Does “Retrofitting” Actually Mean?

Retrofitting refers to installing a home elevator into an existing building, as opposed to incorporating one into a new construction project from the ground up. It’s a different process with its own planning considerations, but modern residential elevator technology has made it significantly more achievable than it was even a decade ago.

The biggest challenge in a retrofit is finding the right lift type for your existing layout — and that’s where working with an experienced supplier who genuinely understands Melbourne’s diverse housing stock makes a real difference.

Melbourne Homes and Lift Compatibility

Not all Melbourne homes are the same, and different lift types suit different structures. Understanding which category your home falls into is the first step in planning a residential elevator installation.

Victorian and Edwardian Terraces

Heritage homes in inner Melbourne suburbs like Carlton, Collingwood, Hawthorn, and Richmond present unique challenges. Many fall within council heritage overlays, which means external structural changes may require council approval before work can begin. For these homes, a shaft-less, no-pit lift — such as the Lift Ease Levare Compact — is often the most practical solution. Because it doesn’t require a traditional shaft or a pit dug into the foundation, the structural impact on the existing building is minimal. Installation can often be completed without significant building works.

If your home is heritage-listed or sits within a heritage overlay, it’s worth confirming with your local council what approvals are required before proceeding. An experienced lift supplier will be able to advise you on how their product’s footprint affects the approval process and what documentation is typically needed.

Post-War and Brick-Veneer Homes

Melbourne’s mid-century homes — particularly those spread across the eastern and south-eastern suburbs — typically offer more flexibility for retrofit home elevator installations. These homes often have more predictable layouts, and a wider range of residential lift models may be suitable depending on available floor space and stairwell configuration.

Homeowners in this category can often choose from hydraulic, traction, or shaft-less systems, and may have more design latitude to make the lift a considered part of the home’s aesthetic rather than simply a functional addition.

Modern Townhouses and Multi-Storey Apartments

Newer multi-storey townhouses and apartments — common across Melbourne’s inner suburbs and outer growth corridors — are among the most straightforward homes to retrofit with a residential elevator. Floor plans are typically more predictable, and structural openings can often be created without affecting load-bearing elements.

For these homes, a premium residential elevator like the Lift Ease Vector Pro can become a genuine design feature: ultra-smooth, whisper-quiet, and built to complement the clean lines of contemporary architecture. It’s the sort of addition that changes how you experience your home every single day.

Planning and Approvals in Melbourne

Before any installation begins, there are a few key regulatory steps Melbourne homeowners should be aware of.

Building Permits

In Victoria, a residential elevator installation will almost always require a building permit. Your lift supplier or installer should be able to advise on whether they manage this as part of their service or whether you’ll need to engage a registered building surveyor separately. At Lift Ease, our team guides clients through this process as part of our full end-to-end service.

Heritage Overlays and Council Requirements

Heritage overlays affect a significant number of Melbourne’s inner-suburb properties. If your home falls within one, your council may have specific requirements regarding changes to the external fabric of the building — particularly if a lift installation involves structural changes to external walls or rooflines. In most cases, shaft-less lift designs that sit entirely within the home’s existing envelope avoid triggering these requirements altogether, making them the natural choice for heritage-sensitive properties.

AS 1735 Compliance

All residential lifts installed in Australia must comply with AS 1735 — the Australian Standard for lifts, escalators, and moving walks. When comparing suppliers, always confirm that their products are certified compliant, and ensure that ongoing servicing and maintenance is performed by qualified technicians to keep the lift in certification. Lift Ease products are fully AS 1735 compliant, and our local team handles servicing across Victoria, plus NSW, SA and Tasmania.

How Long Does a Retrofit Take?

One of the most common concerns Melbourne homeowners raise is disruption to daily life. The good news is that modern shaft-less lifts such as the Levare are typically installed in about 2 days, with minimal mess and no need to vacate the home during the process. More complex installations that involve structural modifications will naturally take a little longer, but experienced suppliers plan the work carefully to minimise impact on your household’s routine.

Lift Ease is proud of its minimal-disruption approach: our clever shaft-less and compact designs mean less structural work, less mess, and faster installation — often completed in days, not weeks.

Is a Home Elevator Right for Your Melbourne Home?

Whether your motivation is accessibility, aging in place, future-proofing a multi-storey home, or simply the everyday practicality of carrying shopping and laundry between floors, a residential elevator installation is a worthwhile investment that pays dividends over the long term. In Melbourne’s competitive property market, it can also add measurable buyer appeal when the time eventually comes to sell.

Every home is different, and the best way to understand what’s genuinely possible for your property is to speak with a specialist who can assess your layout and recommend the most suitable model and installation approach for your specific situation.
