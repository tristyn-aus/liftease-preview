Adding a Home Lift to an Existing House (+ Retrofit Home Lift for Existing Home)

18 July 2026

Most people assume a home lift is something you plan for at the design stage of a new build — not something you add to a house that’s already standing. It’s a fair assumption, but it’s also outdated. Retrofitting a home lift into an existing house is now one of the fastest-growing requests we get at Lift Ease, and for good reason: more Australians are choosing to age in place, care for ageing parents at home, or future-proof a property for a family member with mobility needs, rather than move house entirely.

The good news is that retrofitting a home lift is far more achievable than most homeowners expect. It doesn’t always require major structural rebuilding, and modern low-pit and self-supporting lift systems have made the process significantly simpler than it was even five years ago.

Why Homeowners Retrofit a Home Lift

There are a few common reasons existing homes end up needing a lift added after the fact:

  • Ageing in place — avoiding the upheaval of selling and moving into a single-storey home or aged care facility
  • A change in mobility — following surgery, injury, illness, or a progressive condition
  • Multi-generational living — supporting a parent or relative moving in
  • Long-term property planning — adding accessibility now to avoid a forced, rushed installation later
  • Increasing property value — a well-installed home lift is a genuine selling point in the current market

Can Any Existing House Have a Lift Retrofitted?

In the vast majority of cases, yes. The three main factors we assess are:

  1. Available floor space — most residential lifts need a footprint roughly the size of a large wardrobe, though compact models exist for tighter homes
  2. Structural support — whether the existing floor and ceiling can support the lift shaft, or whether reinforcement is needed
  3. Travel distance — how many levels the lift needs to service, and whether it lines up vertically between floors

Where floors don’t naturally align, or where a full shaft isn’t practical, there are still solutions — including through-floor lifts and self-supporting cabin lifts that don’t require a built shaft at all.

Types of Retrofit Home Lifts

Through-floor home lifts These require the smallest structural intervention. A compact platform travels through a floor opening between two levels, often without needing a full enclosed shaftway, making them one of the most popular retrofit choices.

Self-supporting (freestanding) home lifts Ideal when you don’t want to cut load-bearing structure. The lift’s own frame carries the load, so it can often be installed against an existing wall with minimal building work.

Hydraulic and traction home lifts Best suited to homes with more available space or where a higher-capacity, smoother-ride lift is preferred — common in larger renovations or homes already undergoing structural work.

What the Retrofit Process Looks Like

  1. Site assessment — our team reviews your floor plan and inspects the site to confirm feasibility
  2. Lift selection — matching the right lift type to your home’s layout, budget, and mobility needs
  3. Approvals — we manage the necessary building and compliance documentation
  4. Installation — most residential retrofit installs are completed with far less disruption than homeowners expect
  5. Handover and servicing — full commissioning, safety checks, and an ongoing maintenance plan

How Long Does It Take?

Every home is different, but many retrofit installations are completed within a matter of weeks once approvals are in place, not months. Compact and through-floor models in particular are designed for minimal building disruption.

Is a Retrofit Home Lift Worth the Investment?

For most homeowners, the answer comes down to comparing the cost of a lift against the cost — financial and emotional — of moving house, or the risk of a fall on stairs. A home lift adds independence, safety, and long-term property value, all while letting you stay in the home you already know and love.
