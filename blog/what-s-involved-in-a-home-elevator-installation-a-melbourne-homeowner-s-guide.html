What's Involved in a Home Elevator Installation? A Melbourne Homeowner's Guide

29 June 2026

Whether you’re planning ahead for the years to come, adapting your home for a family member with mobility needs, or simply adding a touch of luxury to a multi-storey renovation — a home elevator is one of the most considered, high-value additions a Melbourne homeowner can make. But what does the process actually look like from start to finish?

This guide walks you through every step, and explains exactly how Lift Ease takes the complexity out of the experience.

Why More Melbourne Homeowners Are Choosing Home Elevators

Melbourne’s housing landscape has changed dramatically. New builds in suburbs like Brighton, Toorak, South Yarra, and Hawthorn are increasingly multi-storey — and older homes undergoing significant renovation are being redesigned for a lifetime of use. A home elevator isn’t a niche product any more. It’s a practical, long-term investment that adds genuine value to your property while transforming daily life.

Motivations vary. Some homeowners are future-proofing — ensuring their home works just as well at 70 as it does today. Others are caring for elderly parents who’ve moved in, or supporting a family member after an injury or diagnosis. And increasingly, homeowners who have simply experienced the daily grind of multiple staircases decide that a residential lift is the most sensible upgrade they can make.

Whatever the reason, the installation process is more straightforward than most people expect. Especially with the right team behind it.

Step 1: The Initial Consultation — Understanding Your Home and Your Needs

Every Lift Ease project starts with a proper conversation. We come to you — no obligation, no sales pressure. We want to understand your home, your lifestyle, and what you’re trying to achieve before we recommend anything.

At this first meeting, a Lift Ease specialist will assess your home’s existing structure and layout to determine the most practical lift placement. We’ll look at the floor plan across all levels, discuss shaft options (or shaft-free solutions where applicable), and talk through your priorities: Is this primarily about accessibility? Convenience? Aesthetic? All three?

We’ll also clarify the range of lift types suited to your home. At Lift Ease, we install a range of residential elevator systems — from sleek, space-efficient pneumatic lifts to traditional cable-driven systems and hydraulic models — and we’ll be honest about which solution best suits your building structure, your usage requirements, and your long-term expectations.

What to expect: A thorough site visit and a clear summary of your options, in plain language. No jargon, no pressure. Most consultations take between 60 and 90 minutes.

Step 2: Design, Specification, and Custom Finishes

Once you’re happy with the recommended solution, we develop a full specification for your lift — including dimensions, structural requirements, finish selections, and any custom features.

This is where the project starts to feel real. Lift Ease produces detailed technical drawings that are submitted to your building surveyor and, where required, your local council. We handle the documentation — you don’t need to navigate that process alone.

On the design side, you’ll choose from a range of cabin finishes, door styles, lighting options, and controls. Whether your home is a contemporary glass-and-steel new build or a period Victorian terrace, a well-designed residential lift should complement the architecture — not fight it.

Step 3: Permits, Approvals, and Compliance

Residential elevator installations in Victoria require building permits and must comply with Australian Standard AS 1735. WorkSafe Victoria also requires registration of any lift installed in a home accessible to the public.

Lift Ease handles this for you. We prepare and lodge the required documentation, liaise directly with your building surveyor, and ensure every aspect of the installation is compliant with AS 1735 (Lifts, Escalators and Moving Walks) and all applicable WorkSafe Victoria requirements. We’ve been through this process dozens of times — it’s routine for us, even when individual projects throw up site-specific complications.

For most standard residential installations in Melbourne, the permit and approval phase takes two to six weeks, depending on the project complexity and your local council.

Step 4: Site Preparation and Structural Work

Before the lift itself arrives, your home may need some preparation work. The extent depends heavily on the lift type selected and your existing structure.

For shaft-based lift systems, a structural shaft needs to be constructed or an existing void adapted. This typically involves a builder or structural carpenter working alongside our team. Lift Ease coordinates this process — we work alongside your builder or can refer you to trusted trades we’ve worked with across Melbourne for years.

For shaft-free pneumatic systems, site preparation is significantly lighter — these units are essentially self-contained and require far less structural modification.

In either case, you’ll need a dedicated power circuit installed for the lift by a licensed electrician. Lift Ease coordinates this too, so you’re not managing multiple contractors independently.

Step 5: The Installation

This is where it all comes together. Installation time depends on the model: Levare lifts take about 2 days, home lifts such as the Vector Pro, Zenith Ultra and Atlus take 1–2 weeks, and commercial lifts (Zenith DDA, E1 and RB150) take 2–4 weeks.

Our installation team are Lift Ease’s own licensed technicians — not subcontractors hired for the job. They know every system we install because they’ve installed them many times before. The work is methodical, clean, and done with respect for your home and your family’s routine.

We’ll confirm access requirements with you beforehand, work efficiently to minimise disruption, and keep you updated throughout. If anything comes up during installation that affects scope or timeline, we’ll tell you immediately — not at the end.

Step 6: Commissioning, Testing, and Final Inspection

Once the lift is physically installed, we commission and test every system — safety devices, door sensors, emergency functions, call controls, and smooth operation across all floors — before we invite any inspection.

A registered lift inspector (required under Victorian law for all residential lifts) then conducts an independent compliance inspection. Lift Ease arranges this inspection and attends it with the inspector. Once the lift passes — and we don’t submit for inspection until we’re confident it will — you receive your compliance certificate and WorkSafe registration.

We don’t consider the job done until the paperwork is in your hand.

Step 7: Handover, Training, and Ongoing Support

At handover, a Lift Ease technician walks you through your new lift — how to operate it, what the safety features do, how to handle a minor issue, and when to call us.

You’ll receive all manufacturer documentation, warranties, and operating manuals in plain language. We’ll also explain your ongoing maintenance obligations — which under Victorian law include annual servicing by a licensed lift mechanic.

Lift Ease offers ongoing service and maintenance plans for all the lifts we install. We’re not a company that installs and disappears. Many of our clients have been with us for years, and we take that relationship seriously.
