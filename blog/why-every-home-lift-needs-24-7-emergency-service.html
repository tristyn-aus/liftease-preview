Why Every Home Lift in Australia Needs a 24/7 Emergency Service Provider

7 December 2025

Imagine this: It’s 9 PM on a Saturday night. You step into your home elevator, press the button for the second floor, and suddenly—silence. The lift stops. The lights dim. You push the button again, but nothing happens. You’re stuck.

This is the moment where the difference between a “standard” lift phone and a professionally monitored 24/7 emergency service becomes critical. In Australia, safety standards are strict for a reason, yet many homeowners are unaware of what their lift’s emergency phone actually connects to.

The Rules: What Does AS1735 Say?

Under Australian Standard AS1735.19, all passenger lifts must be fitted with an emergency communication system. But it’s not enough to just have a phone line. The standard requires that the system:

  • Be permanently installed and hands-free (usually a push-button).
  • Operate even during a power failure (via battery backup).
  • Connect to a location that can provide assistance.

The Critical Loophole

The standard says “assistance,” but it doesn’t strictly define who provides it. Some budget installations simply program the phone to call the homeowner’s mobile or a generic landline. If you are the one stuck in the lift, and the phone calls your mobile (which is in your pocket with no reception inside the metal shaft), no one is coming to help.

The Risks of Unmonitored Systems

  • Calls go to random family members who may be asleep, busy, or unreachable.
  • No technical expertise on the other end of the line.
  • Reliance on standard NBN lines which fail during power outages without specific battery backups.
  • High anxiety: You’re alone with no guarantee of rescue.

The Professional 24/7 Solution

  • Immediate Response: Calls are answered by a trained operator in a dedicated control room, 24/7/365.
  • Dual-Path Technology: Uses mobile GSM networks with battery backup, bypassing NBN power dependencies.
  • Technical Triage: Operators can often diagnose if it’s a false alarm or a genuine emergency.
  • Verified Rescue: They coordinate the technician’s arrival and stay on the line with you.

A Tale of Two Emergencies

Scenario A: The Unmonitored Home

“Sarah, living alone, gets stuck at 11 PM. She presses the lift button. It dials her son’s mobile. He’s at the movies and misses the call. It dials her daughter, who is asleep. Sarah spends 6 hours in the lift until her daughter wakes up and sees the missed call. Panic, dehydration, and trauma ensue.”

Scenario B: The Lift Ease Standard

“John gets stuck at 2 AM. He presses the button. Within 30 seconds, a calm operator speaks: ‘This is the Emergency Control Centre. We see you are at [Address]. Are you okay?’ They confirm John is safe, dispatch an on-call technician immediately, and check on John every 10 minutes until the technician opens the door at 2:45 AM.”

Why “Calling 000” Isn’t the First Solution

Many people assume they should just call the fire brigade (000). While emergency services are amazing, their priority is immediate threat to life (e.g., fire or medical emergency).

If there is no medical emergency, the fire brigade may damage your expensive lift doors to get you out quickly with “jaws of life” tools. A trained lift technician, dispatched by your monitoring provider, has the keys and knowledge to lower the lift manually and open the doors without causing thousands of dollars in damage.
