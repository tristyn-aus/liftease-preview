Does a Home Lift Need Three-Phase Power?

16 November 2026

Short answer: no, a home lift does not need three-phase power. The Vector Pro, Zenith Ultra and Atlus all run on single phase 230VAC, as do the commercial Zenith DDA and the RB150 access platform lift. The only Lift Ease lift that needs three phase is the E1 commercial elevator.

That matters for most Victorian homes and many smaller commercial buildings, because a three-phase upgrade is often the step people worry about when they first start planning a lift. If you are choosing a home elevator, or a DDA lift for a lower-rise building, you can usually work with the single-phase supply that is already there.

All of these lifts are machine-room-less. The Vector Pro’s pump fits in a small cupboard; the others do not need a cupboard or a machine room either. See MRL vs machine room lifts if you are also planning the space around the shaft.

Home elevators: single phase is standard

If you are putting a lift into a house or townhouse, plan around single phase 230VAC. That is the published power supply for all three of our full home elevators:

  • The Vector Pro is a hydraulic home elevator with a published current draw of around 14 A, depending on configuration.
  • The Zenith Ultra is an advanced belt-drive home elevator. It also runs on single phase 230VAC and includes an energy recovery system.
  • The Atlus is an electric hybrid screw drive. It runs on single phase 230VAC, uses no oil, and has a backup battery drive as standard.

Our comparison of Vector Pro, Zenith Ultra and Atlus covers pit depth, stops, load and doors if you are still choosing a model. For the building work itself, see the home lift planning guide.

Commercial buildings: only the E1 needs three phase

A commercial lift does not automatically mean a three-phase upgrade. Many of our commercial solutions run on single phase, which can avoid an expensive three-phase electrical upgrade and keep the install simpler.

  • The Zenith DDA is the public-use version of the Zenith. It is a machine-room-less electric traction lift, fully DDA and NCC compliant, and it runs on single phase 230VAC. It serves up to 10 levels (G+9).
  • The RB150 is an access platform lift for shorter rises (600 mm to 3,000 mm). It also runs on single phase 230VAC, and installation is within a week.
  • The E1 is the model that needs three phase. It is a VVVF traction lift for low and mid-rise developments, with speeds of 1.0 to 2.0 m/s, travel of up to 35 m, and a rated load of up to 2,500 kg.

If you are weighing those options, start with Zenith DDA, E1 or RB150 and our commercial and DDA lifts page. Builders can also see lifts for builders.

What happens when the power goes out?

A power cut should not leave someone stranded. Home lifts have a battery backup that brings the car to the nearest landing and opens the door. The Atlus also has a backup battery drive. Our commercial lifts have battery backup for safe emergency lowering during a power failure, plus an integrated auto-dialler and alarm system.

Regular elevator servicing includes checks of the electrical system and emergency features, including battery backup and alarm systems. Our team also services and repairs lifts from all major manufacturers.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Do I need three-phase power for a home lift?

No. Every Lift Ease home elevator — the Vector Pro, Zenith Ultra and Atlus — runs on single phase 230VAC.

Which commercial lifts need three-phase power?

Only the E1 needs three phase. The Zenith DDA and the RB150 access platform lift both run on single phase 230VAC.

How much power does a Vector Pro use?

The Vector Pro's published current draw is around 14 A, depending on configuration. It still runs on single phase 230VAC.

What happens if the power goes out?

Home lifts have a battery backup that brings the car to the nearest landing and opens the door. The Atlus also has a backup battery drive. Our commercial lifts have battery backup for safe emergency lowering during a power failure.

Does a lift also need a machine room for the motor?

No. Every Lift Ease lift is machine-room-less, so you don't need to find or build a machine room.

Do you cover areas outside Melbourne?

We work mainly across Victoria, plus selected projects in NSW, SA and Tasmania.
