The Best Home Lift for a Small Townhouse (And Why Retrofitting in Melbourne Is Easier Than You Think)

20 July 2026

If you live in a narrow Melbourne townhouse, you’ve probably had this thought while climbing the stairs with your arms full of groceries: there has to be a better way to move between floors.

There is. Home lifts are no longer reserved for sprawling architect-designed houses. Compact, retrofit-friendly models built specifically for tight footprints now make it possible to install a lift in almost any small townhouse — often without a full renovation, a concrete shaft, or months of construction.

This guide covers what to actually look for in a home lift for a small townhouse, why Melbourne’s townhouse stock is particularly well suited to retrofit lifts, and which Lift Ease models tend to be the best fit.

Why Small Townhouses Need a Different Kind of Lift

Most townhouses built over the last two decades share the same constraints:

  • Narrow floor plates, often 4–6 metres wide
  • Tight stairwells with little to no spare structural space
  • Limited services access — no existing shaft, void, or lift well
  • Multi-level layouts (2–3 storeys) common in Melbourne’s medium-density developments

A commercial-style lift designed for apartment buildings simply won’t fit — and doesn’t need to. What a small townhouse needs is a low-footprint, self-supporting home lift that installs into existing space without structural excavation.

What to Look For in a Home Lift for a Small Townhouse

1. A Small Footprint

The best small-townhouse lifts typically require as little as 0.8–1.1 square metres of floor space per level — comparable to a large linen cupboard. Look for a self-supporting frame rather than a design that requires a built shaft.

2. No Pit or Minimal Pit Requirement

Traditional lifts need a dug-out pit below ground level. Many modern home lifts (including screw-drive and pneumatic-style systems) need no pit at all, or only a shallow recess of a few centimetres — critical for retrofits where you can’t excavate under a slab.

3. Low Headroom Requirements

Older townhouses often have limited roof clearance above the top landing. A good compact lift will operate with reduced overhead clearance rather than requiring extra roof works.

4. Standard Power Supply

Look for a lift that runs on a standard single-phase power supply, so you’re not paying for an electrical upgrade on top of the lift itself.

5. Quiet, Low-Vibration Operation

In a small home, the lift shaft is often adjacent to a bedroom or living area. Hydraulic or screw-drive systems tend to be significantly quieter than older traction designs.

6. Compliance with Australian Standards

Any home lift installed in Australia should meet AS 1735 (lift standards) and relevant state building requirements. This matters even more in a retrofit, where an installer needs to adapt a compliant design to an existing structure — not the other way around.

Why Retrofit Home Lifts Work So Well in Melbourne

Melbourne’s housing mix makes it one of the best cities in Australia for home lift retrofits, for a few reasons:

  • A large stock of established townhouses and double-storey homes, many now owned by residents wanting to age in place
  • Consistent, predictable weather for external lift shaft installations where needed
  • Established trades and compliance pathways through Victorian building surveyors familiar with home lift approvals
  • Growing demand for accessibility retrofits, driven by both an ageing population and NDIS-related home modifications

A retrofit lift installation in an existing Melbourne townhouse typically takes days, not months, when the right compact system is chosen. Installers core-drill floor penetrations, position the self-supporting frame, and connect standard power — without opening up walls or re-engineering the roofline.

Best Lift Ease Models for Small Townhouses

Lift Ease designs and installs home lifts across Melbourne, South Australia, Tasmania, and New South Wales, with several models suited to compact, retrofit applications:

Levare Compact — Purpose-built for small floor plates, the Levare Compact is our narrowest-footprint model, designed for townhouses and terrace homes where every centimetre of floor space counts.

Levare Plus — A step up in cabin size while still fitting comfortably into typical townhouse stairwell voids, suited to homes wanting a slightly larger, wheelchair-considerate cabin.

Vector Pro — For townhouses with a little more room to work with, offering a larger cabin and higher weight capacity without requiring a full shaft build.

Each model can be configured for retrofit installation, meaning no structural shaft is required in most cases — just an assessment of your existing stairwell, void, or corner space.

What a Retrofit Installation Process Looks Like

  1. Site assessment — A Lift Ease technician measures your available space, floor structure, and power supply.
  2. Model selection — Based on footprint, number of levels, and budget, we recommend the right Levare model.
  3. Compliance and approvals — We manage the paperwork required under Victorian building regulations.
  4. Installation — Most retrofit installations are completed in a matter of days with minimal disruption to the rest of the home.
  5. Handover and servicing — Your lift is commissioned, tested, and set up on an ongoing maintenance schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lift shaft built for a small townhouse retrofit? In most cases, no. Compact home lift models are self-supporting and install into existing voids, stairwells, or corner spaces without a concrete shaft.

How much floor space does a small home lift actually need? As little as roughly one square metre per level, depending on the model and cabin size chosen.

Can a home lift be installed without digging a pit? Yes — many compact retrofit lifts require no pit, or only a shallow recess, making them suitable for homes on a concrete slab.

How long does installation take? A typical townhouse retrofit installation can be completed in a matter of days once approvals are in place.

Is a home lift a good option for ageing in place? Yes. A compact home lift is one of the most effective modifications for maintaining independence and safe access between floors as mobility needs change.
