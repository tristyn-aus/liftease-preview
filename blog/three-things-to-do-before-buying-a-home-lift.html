Three Things to Do Before Buying a Home Lift

24 June 2026

Buying a home lift is one of the most significant investments you can make in your property. Done right, it adds lasting value, comfort, and accessibility to your home for decades to come. Done wrong — with the wrong company, the wrong product, or without the right questions asked — it can become an expensive and stressful ordeal.

At Lift Ease, we’ve guided hundreds of Australian homeowners through the process, and there are three things we always recommend doing before you commit to any lift company. Get these right and you’ll be in a much stronger position to make a confident, well-informed decision.

1. Visit a Showroom — Do Not Buy Without Seeing the Lift in Person

This is the single most important step on this list. Before you spend tens of thousands of dollars on a home lift, you must physically visit a showroom and see the elevator with your own eyes.

We cannot stress this enough: do not buy a home lift from a company that doesn’t have a physical showroom or place of business you can visit.

Unfortunately, there are real risks in today’s market. With home lifts growing in popularity across Australia, more operators have entered the space — and not all of them are reputable. Some companies exist only online, import low-cost units, and disappear after the sale. Others have no local support infrastructure, meaning if something goes wrong after installation, you’re on your own.

Visiting a showroom protects you in several important ways:

Any reputable lift company will welcome a showroom visit. If a company discourages you from visiting or doesn’t have a showroom at all, that should be a serious red flag. The risk of a scam is simply not worth it. Visit first, buy second — every time.

Lift Ease has a showroom where you can view our full range of residential elevators and speak directly with our team. We encourage every prospective customer to come in before making any decision.

2. Ask About After-Sales Support and the Technical Service Team

The sale of a home lift is just the beginning of the relationship. A quality residential elevator will serve your home for 20 years or more — and over that time, it will need routine servicing, occasional maintenance, and sometimes repair. The company you buy from needs to be there for all of it.

Before you sign anything, ask directly: What does your after-sales support look like, and what is your service team’s experience?

Here’s what you want to hear in the answer:

A company with a strong, experienced service division is telling you something important: they’re built for the long term. They’re not just selling units and moving on — they’re invested in the ongoing performance of every lift they install.

This matters enormously for your peace of mind and the safety of everyone in your home.

3. Ask How the Project Timeline Is Structured — and Who Owns Each Stage

Installing a home lift isn’t a same-day purchase. It involves multiple departments, a manufacturing or procurement lead time, site preparation, installation, and a formal handover process. Before you commit, you deserve to understand exactly what that journey looks like — and who is responsible for it at every step.

Here are the questions to ask your lift company upfront:

A professional lift company will have clear, documented answers to all of these questions. They should be able to give you a realistic timeline for each stage and commit to keeping you informed throughout. Vague timelines and unclear ownership of different stages are a warning sign — they often indicate a disorganised operation that will cause frustration down the track.

At Lift Ease, we walk every customer through the full project timeline before they sign. You’ll know exactly what happens at each stage, who to call, and what to expect.

Making the Right Choice the First Time

Buying a home lift is a big decision, and you only want to make it once. Take the time to visit showrooms, ask the hard questions about service capability, and make sure you understand the full project process before you sign.

The right lift company will welcome your scrutiny. They’ll have clear answers, real infrastructure, and a track record you can verify.
