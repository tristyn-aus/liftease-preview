Apartment Elevators in Melbourne: What Strata Committees and Developers Need to Know

14 July 2026

Melbourne’s apartment market has never been more dynamic. From compact one-bedroom developments in Southbank and Docklands to boutique mid-rise buildings in Richmond, Fitzroy, and Brunswick, vertical living is reshaping how Melburnians think about home ownership. But for developers, strata committees, and building managers, the question of elevators — whether to install one, how to retrofit one into an existing building, or how to ensure a current lift continues to meet compliance — is one of the most consequential decisions an apartment building can face.

This guide covers everything Melbourne strata committees, owners corporations, and developers need to know: from compliance obligations and installation options to body corporate approval processes and how to choose the right elevator for your building’s specific needs.

Why Melbourne Apartment Buildings Need Elevators

In any multi-storey apartment building, elevators are not optional additions — they are a practical necessity and, in many cases, a legal requirement. Under the National Construction Code (NCC), residential apartment buildings of a certain height and classification must provide accessible vertical circulation for all residents and visitors, including those with mobility impairments.

Beyond the legal baseline, the demand for accessible apartments in Melbourne is growing. With the city’s population ageing and accessible housing chronically undersupplied, buildings that offer reliable, well-maintained elevator access are significantly more attractive to tenants and buyers. A functioning, modern elevator is no longer a premium feature — it is an expectation.

For older Melbourne apartment stock — particularly walk-up buildings from the 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s constructed without lifts — the absence of an elevator is becoming both a competitive and a compliance liability. Retrofitting a lift into these buildings is increasingly common, and the process, while complex, is well understood and achievable.

Compliance and Legal Requirements for Apartment Elevators in Melbourne

All elevators installed in Melbourne apartment buildings must comply with AS 1735, the Australian Standard governing lift design, installation, and ongoing maintenance. Compliance is non-negotiable for any new installation or major upgrade.

Under Victorian law, lift owners — which in a strata context means the owners corporation — are responsible for ensuring the building’s elevator is maintained in safe working order and inspected regularly by a licensed lift mechanic. Victoria requires at minimum annual servicing, though most lift suppliers recommend more frequent maintenance for elevators under heavy daily use.

The Disability Discrimination Act (DDA) also applies to common areas of apartment buildings. While the DDA does not impose a blanket retrofit obligation on existing buildings, failing to provide accessible vertical transport can expose an owners corporation to discrimination complaints — particularly where the building is undertaking significant renovation or capital works.

Installing Elevators in Melbourne Apartment Buildings: New Builds vs Retrofits

New Melbourne Apartment Developments

For developers building new apartment complexes in Melbourne, the elevator decision should happen at the earliest possible stage of design. Integrating the lift shaft into the building’s structural drawings from the outset is significantly less costly than accommodating one later, and gives the design team full flexibility over shaft placement, dimensions, and the number of floors served.

Under the NCC, Class 2 residential apartment buildings generally need to provide accessible paths of travel, and in taller buildings this means elevator access is required as a matter of compliance. Planning for this early — and selecting a lift supplier with apartment development experience — is the most efficient path to a compliant, well-integrated result.

Retrofitting Elevators into Existing Melbourne Apartment Buildings

Retrofitting an elevator into an existing Melbourne apartment building is more complex, but far from uncommon. Older walk-up buildings across inner Melbourne — Prahran, St Kilda, Collingwood, Carlton — are increasingly seeking retrofit solutions to meet modern accessibility expectations and protect asset value.

The key challenges in a retrofit project are structural: identifying a viable shaft location, assessing load-bearing capacity, and managing disruption to residents during construction. A thorough structural assessment is the essential first step. Lift Ease works closely with structural engineers and builders to develop retrofit plans that minimise impact on the building and its occupants.

Retrofit projects in heritage-listed buildings or those within Melbourne’s heritage overlay zones require additional planning, as structural changes must be assessed for heritage impact before a building permit can be issued.

Strata and Body Corporate Considerations for Melbourne Apartment Elevators

For strata-titled apartment buildings in Victoria, installing or replacing an elevator is a significant capital works decision requiring owners corporation approval. Under the Owners Corporations Act 2006 (Vic), decisions of this scale typically require a special resolution — meaning support from 75% of lot entitlements represented at a general meeting.

Funding is the other major consideration. Elevator installations are typically funded through a special levy across all lot owners, drawdown from the building’s sinking fund, or a facility loan secured against the owners corporation. Strata managers and financial advisors can help model the most appropriate structure for a specific building.

It is also worth noting that the elevator, once installed, becomes common property — meaning the owners corporation is responsible for its ongoing maintenance, insurance, and eventual replacement. These long-term obligations should be factored into financial planning before any decision is made.

Lift Ease works regularly with strata managers, building managers, and owners corporations across Melbourne, and can provide the technical documentation and specifications needed to support a body corporate approval process.

Choosing the Right Elevator for Your Melbourne Apartment Building

The right elevator depends on the number of floors, expected passenger volume, structural constraints, and the level of finish appropriate for the building’s market positioning.

For boutique apartment buildings of three to five storeys with modest daily usage, a mid-range traction or hydraulic elevator will typically meet both compliance and performance requirements. For larger Melbourne developments — high-rise buildings, mixed-use complexes, or aged care facilities where elevator demand is intensive and sustained — a higher-specification commercial system is the appropriate choice.
