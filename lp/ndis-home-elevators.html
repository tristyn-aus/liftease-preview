Funding

NDIS and Support at Home, in brief

NDIS

NDIS home modification funding can cover a lift for eligible participants where it's assessed as reasonable and necessary. If a home lift is approved, funding is included under Capital Supports, specifically the Home Modifications budget within your plan.

Generally you will need an OT report, a formal quote from a qualified installer and a structural review. Approval can take several months, so it's worth starting early.

Support at Home (AT-HM)

If you're approved for Support at Home, the AT-HM scheme can contribute up to $15,390 towards prescribed home modifications. It's a lifetime amount, an OT has to prescribe the work, and you'll usually pay a contribution based on your pension status.

Funding will rarely cover the whole cost of a home lift; it usually goes towards the lift and you cover the balance.

FAQs

Quick answers

Can the NDIS pay for a home elevator?

Yes, in the right circumstances. The NDIS treats a home lift as a complex home modification, funded through the Capital Supports, Home Modifications budget, provided it meets the scheme's funding criteria and is supported by the right evidence.

What evidence do I need for NDIS elevator funding?

Generally an occupational therapist report confirming the lift is necessary, a formal quote from a qualified installer, and a structural review. More complex projects may also involve a building construction practitioner or independent project manager.

Can I get funding for a home lift through Support at Home?

Possibly. AT-HM can put up to $15,390 towards prescribed home modifications. Stair lifts are a named example; a home lift depends on the AT-HM list and your OT's prescription. The funding usually covers part of the cost, not all of it.

Can I choose which lift company does the work?

Your provider sources the work, but you can ask them to get a quote from a particular supplier. If you self-manage, you can choose suppliers with your provider's agreement.
