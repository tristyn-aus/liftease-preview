Funding

NDIS and Support at Home funding for a home lift

NDIS

NDIS home modification funding can cover a lift for eligible participants where it's assessed as reasonable and necessary. If a home lift is approved, funding is included under Capital Supports, specifically the Home Modifications budget within your plan.

NDIS home lift funding guide →

Support at Home (AT-HM)

You may be able to get government funding towards a home lift, but it will rarely cover the whole cost. If you're approved for the Support at Home program, the Assistive Technology and Home Modifications (AT-HM) scheme can contribute up to $15,390 towards changes that make your home safer to get around. An occupational therapist (OT) has to prescribe the work.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Can't see your question? Call 1300 81 01 47 or read our planning guide.

Can you put a lift in an existing house?

Yes, a large percentage of home lifts are retrofitted.

Can a home lift fit a wheelchair?

Yes, provided the cabin is specified for it from the start. A car that takes a wheelchair and a carer needs internal dimensions of roughly 1100mm by 1400mm, which is noticeably bigger than a standard two person cabin and needs a larger shaft to suit. Retrofitting a bigger car later is rarely practical, so raise wheelchair access at the first site visit. NDIS home modification funding can cover a lift for eligible participants where it's assessed as reasonable and necessary.

What happens if the power goes out?

Home lifts have a battery backup that brings the car to the nearest landing and opens the door, so nobody is left sitting in a stopped lift.

Does a home lift need a pit?

Most modern home lifts need only a shallow pit, somewhere between 30mm and 200mm below the lowest floor. Some models run with no pit at all using a small ramp into the car.

Do I need council approval to install a home lift?

A building permit is required in most cases, because cutting an opening through a floor is structural work. That means a building surveyor, and usually engineering certification for the floor opening. Permits and certification typically add $1,000 to $3,000 to the project. Heritage overlays, external shafts and owners corporations each add another step, so it's worth starting the approvals early rather than after the lift is ordered.

What maintenance is required?

We recommend a minimum of one service per year, twice-yearly for heavily used lifts, or as outlined by the manufacturer's servicing schedule, to keep your elevator in peak condition. This includes lubricating moving parts, checking safety systems, and ensuring all components are functioning correctly. We offer comprehensive service packages for your peace of mind.
