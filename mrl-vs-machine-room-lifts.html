Lift design compared

MRL vs machine room lifts: what your building needs to plan for

Quick answer

A machine room lift keeps its motor, controller and drive equipment in a separate room, usually above or beside the shaft. A machine-room-less (MRL) lift fits that equipment inside the shaft or lift tower.

MRL designs free up floor and roof space and mean less building work, which is why they suit new apartments, offices and homes. Many of our home lifts need no machine room at all.

Why machine rooms existed

Traditional traction lifts put their motor and sheave in a room above the shaft, and older hydraulic lifts put their pump and tank in a room beside it. Keeping the machinery outside the shaft made it easy to reach, but it costs floor space and adds building work.

What changed with MRL

More compact motors and drives can now fit inside the shaft itself. Machine-room-less and shallow-pit designs mean less excavation and structural work, faster installs and lower construction costs, which is why they are increasingly popular in apartment developments.

Home lifts have gone the same way. The Atlus is a fully contained screw system, the Levare range is shaft-less with no pit, and even our hydraulic Vector Pro needs only a small cupboard for its pump, thanks to its submerged worm screw hydraulic motor.

Choosing

Which suits your project?

MRL suits you if

  • Floor or roof space is valuable, as in most new apartments and homes.
  • You want less excavation and structural work.
  • You are retrofitting a lift into an existing home.

A machine room may suit you if

  • The building already has a machine room you can reuse.
  • Your designer or builder has planned for one from the start.

Common misconceptions

Myths debunked

  • Myth Every lift needs a machine room.

    Fact Many modern lifts are machine-room-less, with the drive inside the shaft or lift tower. The Atlus is a fully contained screw system, and even our hydraulic Vector Pro needs only a small cupboard for its pump, with no machine room.

  • Myth A home lift needs a deep pit.

    Fact Most modern home lifts need only a shallow pit. The Atlus needs 50 to 150 mm, and the Levare Compact and Levare Plus need no pit or shaft at all. We confirm the exact pit for your model at the site visit.

  • Myth You can’t add a lift to an existing house.

    Fact A large share of home lifts are retrofitted. Shallow-pit and shaft-less designs such as the Atlus and the Levare range are made for existing homes.

FAQs

Questions we get asked

What does MRL mean?

Machine-room-less. The lift’s motor and drive sit inside the shaft or lift tower, so no separate machine room is needed.

Does a home lift need a machine room?

Many don’t. The Atlus is fully contained, the Levare range is shaft-less, and the hydraulic Vector Pro needs only a small cupboard for its pump.

Does a hydraulic lift always need a machine room?

Older hydraulic designs usually did. The Vector Pro uses a submerged worm screw hydraulic motor, so its pump needs only a small cupboard, with no machine room.

Is an MRL lift harder to service?

It is serviced differently. With no machine room, technicians reach the drive from within the shaft. Regular servicing, at least once a year, keeps any lift safe and reliable.

Do Lift Ease commercial lifts need a machine room?

It depends on the model and building. We confirm it in your commercial proposal, typically within 48 hours.

Do MRL lifts still need a pit?

Usually a shallow one, depending on the model. The Atlus needs 50 to 150 mm and the Levare range needs none.
